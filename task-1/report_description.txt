Project Report Description: Task 1 - Data Cleaning and Preliminary Analysis with R

For the initial week of my Virtual R Data Analyst Internship with Yuva Intern, I carried out a complete data cleaning, preprocessing, and preliminary exploratory analysis on the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset, which comprises 7,043 customer accounts and 21 distinct attributes spanning demographics, account subscriptions, and billing histories. 

During the initial data audit, I uncovered that the TotalCharges attribute had been parsed as a character string instead of a continuous numeric variable due to 11 blank whitespace values. Rather than discarding these observations or using an arbitrary mean, I cross-referenced them against account tenure and verified that every single one belonged to newly onboarded customers with zero months of service. Consequently, imputing zero for their cumulative billing accurately preserved data integrity without distorting downstream calculations. 

I next audited numerical features for anomalies using Tukey's 1.5 x IQR standard. While the data spans wide operational ranges, no values represented data entry defects; instead, attributes like tenure and monthly charges displayed natural multimodal customer grouping. To prepare the features for subsequent statistical testing and machine learning, I applied a natural logarithmic transformation (log1p) to TotalCharges to counteract its heavy rightward skew (+0.96), standardized continuous attributes using both Min-Max and Z-score methods, and encoded categorical variables through ordinal ranking and one-hot dummy matrices.

The exploratory data analysis revealed a baseline churn rate of 26.54%. Bivariate cross-tabulations and Pearson correlations (-0.352 with tenure) clearly demonstrated that customer retention is heavily tied to account longevity and contractual commitment. Customers on month-to-month plans exhibited a 42.71% churn rate, compared to a mere 2.83% among two-year subscribers. Furthermore, accounts utilizing Fiber Optic connections and Electronic Check payments showed noticeably higher attrition, pointing to pricing friction and payment automation as major operational risk areas. The accompanying report provides full documentation, structured R scripts, output tables, and graphical evidence.
